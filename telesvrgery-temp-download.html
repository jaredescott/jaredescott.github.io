Haptic XR Teleoperation of Intravascular
Procedure Devices via VR Glove Physics Interface
AWE XR 2026  ·  Medical Robotics & Extended Reality  ·  Real-Time Closed-Loop Control
We present a closed-loop teleoperation system that translates immersive VR hand physics — captured through haptic gloves — directly into dual-axis motor commands for a physical catheter/guidewire drive device. A virtual cylinder in Unity (SteamVR + HaptX) serves as a physics-based proxy instrument: the operator physically grabs and manipulates it, encoding axial translation and rotational twist as absolute setpoints. These are transmitted at ~20 Hz via USB serial to an Arduino Mega running independent PID controllers, enabling intuitive, scale-calibrated, haptic-informed teleoperation of physical interventional instruments with no line-of-sight constraints.

Novel Contributions
Key System Capabilities
01
Physics-emergent haptic input — HaptX glove forces act on a Rigidbody cylinder; translation and twist arise from physical contact, not from gesture recognition or button mappings.
02
Continuous unwrapped twist tracking — Quaternion decomposition with local-Euler fallback eliminates ±180° discontinuities; configurable axis-alignment threshold handles non-ideal HaptX poses.
03
Real-world diameter calibration — SizeChanger maps virtual cylinder scale to physical catheter/guidewire diameters (e.g. 6.5 mm), preserving spatial fidelity across instrument sizes.
04
Robust serial I/O at motor load — Byte-buffered RX drain on both host and Mega prevents packet loss when PID/motor work delays the Arduino loop; deferred echo eliminates TX/RX contention.
05
Decoupled calibration modes — Visual recenter (C) and firmware zero (F10) are fully independent: operators reset the VR view without disrupting absolute motor position mapping.
